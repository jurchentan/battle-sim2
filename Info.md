# Battle Map Editor

## Project Overview & Product Vision

### The Idea

Battle Map Editor is a creative tool for drawing historical-style battle maps and showing how a battle changes over time. Users create the terrain themselves, organize armies into brigades and larger formations, place troops on the battlefield, and build a sequence of battle phases.

The result is a set of clear, illustrated maps that can be revised and reused without redrawing everything.

The look is inspired by traditional military-history maps: muted green forests, pale fields, winding roads and rivers, contour lines, bold formation lines, commander names, defensive positions, and arrows indicating troop movements.

It is not limited to the American Civil War; it can be used for Napoleonic, ancient, fictional, or other battle settings.

### Who It Is For

The tool is intended for military-history enthusiasts, educators, authors, wargame scenario designers, and creators making battle explanations or visual stories.

### Core Features

**1. Terrain Map Creation**

Users start with a blank canvas and manually draw forests, fields, hills, rivers, roads, bridges, settlements, and other geographical features. Everything remains editable.

**2. Reusable Map Library**

Terrain maps can be saved, reopened, edited, duplicated, and reused for multiple battles. Users do not need to recreate the same battlefield for each scenario.

**3. Army and Brigade Management**

Users create opposing factions and organize military formations through a hierarchy of armies, corps, divisions, and brigades. Each formation can have its own commander, strength, type, and notes.

**4. Interactive Troop Deployment**

Brigades are represented primarily by colored military formation lines. Users can place, rotate, move, resize, label, and hide formations.

**5. Battle Phases**

Users can create a sequence of phases, each showing different troop positions, strengths, commanders, statuses, and tactical movements. Earlier phases remain unchanged when a later phase is edited.

**6. Map Exporting**

Completed battle maps can be exported as high-quality images for historical articles, educational materials, presentations, and videos.

### Typical User Experience

A user draws a fictional river valley, including forests, hills, several roads, and a village. They save this landscape in their personal map library.

Next, they create two opposing armies, defining their commanders, divisions, brigades, and strengths.

They create a new battle scenario using their saved terrain and army rosters, then drag formations into their starting positions.

After saving the initial deployment, they duplicate the phase, move several brigades forward, and draw arrows illustrating an attack. They repeat this for subsequent phases, showing counterattacks and retreats.

Finally, they switch through the completed sequence and export a map of each phase.

### User Interface and Visual Style

The application should resemble a clean, professional mapmaking workspace.

The map occupies most of the screen. Drawing tools are positioned to the left, selected object properties to the right, and a timeline of battle phases along the bottom.

Maps should use an illustrated historical-cartography style, with subdued terrain colors and clearly distinguishable military formations.

Faction colors, labels, and formation styles must be customizable. The tool should work equally well for historical and fictional armies.

### Three Reusable Components

**Terrain Maps:** The battlefield's geography, stored independently of any military scenario.

**Army Rosters:** Factions, commanders, units, strengths, and chains of command, stored independently from terrain.

**Battle Scenarios:** A combination of terrain and armies, including troop deployments, battle phases, and tactical annotations.

Each scenario should contain its own copy of the selected terrain and armies, ensuring that later edits to the originals do not unexpectedly change existing scenarios.

### Initial Project Scope

The first version should prioritize a complete creation workflow: drawing and saving terrain, organizing armies, placing brigades, switching battle phases, saving scenarios, and exporting finished images.

It does not need Google Maps, imported geographic data, automatic combat simulation, AI-generated battle outcomes, multiplayer editing, or 3D terrain.

Animated battle playback, branching timelines, temporary phase-only battle lines, AI-assisted terrain creation, and additional map styles may be considered in later versions.

### What Success Looks Like

The project succeeds when someone can create a battlefield from scratch, save and reuse it, organize dozens of military formations, illustrate the progression of a battle over several phases, and produce clear, professional-looking maps.

**The ultimate goal is to make historical-style battle map creation as accessible and editable as making a slide presentation, while preserving the military organization and narrative of the battle.**
