# M5.6 plan — battle line deployment tool

Status: implemented (2026-11-25). Acceptance criteria verified; see `HANDOFF.md`
for the final gate results.

Follow-up (2026-11-28): **Flip 180°**, **Remove selected from map**, **Draw/Cancel
battle line**, and **Place on map** now share one **Bulk actions** section in the
inspector, replacing the toolbar menu and the separate contextual shortcut.
Deleting a formation asks for confirmation, cascades through its entire
descendant subtree, and removes every deleted unit's phase states and annotation
links in one undo entry.

Scope: extend the M5.5 scenario builder with a freehand **battle line** tool that
places brigades along a drawn line, plus the bulk formation actions needed to
adjust the result. The tool works on the active phase, uses the existing
selection and undo model, and adds no new document fields or migrations.

## Confirmed decisions

- The user selects a formation group (division/corps/army) **or any set of
  brigades**, then presses **Draw battle line** and drags a freehand line.
- Selecting a higher formation resolves to all of its descendant brigades.
  Selecting individual brigades directly is supported, including two brigades
  from two different divisions.
- The tool **deploys undeployed brigades and repositions already-deployed
  brigades** in the same action. Commands inside the selected formation's tree
  deploy with them and sit behind their brigade groups.
- Brigades from the same division stay next to each other. Division groups are
  ordered by the order of battle, not by click order.
- If the drawn line is shorter than the brigades need, the brigades **overlap**
  rather than being rejected or resized.
- **Flip 180° rotates every deployed selected formation in place.** Positions and
  order do not change; labels move to the other side.
- Deployed division/corps/army labels **recenter behind their brigade group**
  when the whole group is part of the line.
- The line itself is a transient drawing guide. Only the resulting unit states
  are saved; no annotation is created.
- Add bulk formation actions for multi-selection: **bulk frontage** and a
  general **Flip 180°** action. Existing bulk rotation and removal remain, and
  all four scenario bulk actions live in one inspector section.
- Every draw, flip, and bulk edit is one undoable history entry.

## 1. User flow

```text
Order of battle mode
  ├─ Select Voss Division (or Shift-click several brigades)
  ├─ Click "Draw battle line"
  ├─ Drag a freehand line across the map
  └─ On release:
       ├─ Voss Division's brigades deploy shoulder-to-shoulder along the line
       ├─ Same-division brigades stay contiguous
       ├─ Deployed Voss Division label moves behind its brigades
       └─ The placed formations become the selection

Inspector (formation or multi-selection)
  └─ Bulk actions
       ├─ "Flip 180°"            rotates deployed selected formations in place
       ├─ "Remove selected from map"
       ├─ "Draw battle line"     arms the tool; disabled without brigades
       └─ "Place on map"         single undeployed formation only
```

- The draw button is available from the inspector whenever the selection
  resolves to at least one brigade, and stays enabled while armed so it can
  cancel. Its tooltip explains how to make it available.
- Flip and Remove are enabled while at least one selected formation is deployed
  in the active phase. Place on map is enabled only for one undeployed formation.
- While the tool is armed, the cursor is a crosshair and the canvas hint reads
  “Drag to draw the battle line · Esc cancels”.
- Zoom and pan before starting the stroke. The stroke captures the current
  view; mid-stroke panning is out of scope.
- Escape cancels the armed tool or an in-progress stroke without changing the
  document.

## 2. Selection resolution

Add a pure resolver in `features/scenario/battleLine.ts`.

Inputs: roster, selected unit IDs, hidden faction IDs.

1. For every selected unit, include the unit and all of its descendants.
   Selecting a brigade contributes just that brigade.
2. Deduplicate IDs.
3. Keep only existing brigades whose faction is visible. Hidden formations are
   not placed; if a formation state is already hidden, its visibility is
   preserved when it is moved.
4. Group brigades by their **nearest division ancestor**. A brigade with no
   division ancestor falls back to its parent unit, or to an “independent”
   group when it has no parent.
5. Order groups by depth-first order of battle order. Order brigades inside a
   group by the existing `unitChildren` order (name/id), so results are
   deterministic and independent of click order.

Examples:

- Select **Voss Division** → its three brigades, one group.
- Select **I Corps** → Voss Division's brigades, then any other division
  brigades under the corps, grouped by division.
- Shift-click **Voss 1st Brigade** and **Crown 2nd Brigade** → two groups, one
  brigade each, ordered by the order of battle.
- Select a brigade and its division → the division's brigades are included once.

If the resolver returns no brigades, the draw button stays disabled with an
explanation such as “Select a formation with brigades, or select brigades
directly.”

## 3. Freehand line capture

- Add transient editor state:
  - `battleLineActive: boolean`
  - `lastBattleLine: { unitIds: string[]; brigadeIds: string[]; points: Point[] } | null`
- The draft stroke itself is component-local in `ScenarioCanvas` so pointer
  moves do not re-render the whole workspace.
- While active:
  - Formation shapes, the transformer, marquee selection, and single-placement
    clicks are disabled.
  - Left pointer-down starts the stroke; pointer moves append world-space
    points; pointer-up finishes it.
  - Sample a point only after a small screen-space movement threshold so the
    stroke does not collect hundreds of duplicate points.
  - Simplify the finished stroke with the existing `simplifyPoints` helper.
  - Reject only a degenerate stroke (fewer than two usable points or less than
    a small world-space length, e.g. 8 units) with an inline message; stay
    armed so the user can try again.
- Render a live preview: the freehand line plus small perpendicular ticks at
  the planned brigade centres, coloured by faction. The actual unit shapes are
  committed on pointer-up.
- On success, compute the layout, apply it, store `lastBattleLine`, select the
  placed formations, and leave the tool.
- Expose test attributes on the canvas for E2E: `data-battle-line-active`,
  `data-battle-line-brigade-count`, `data-battle-line-draft-points`, and
  `data-last-battle-line-count`. Inspector actions use
  `data-testid="formation-actions"`, `data-testid="bulk-flip-180"`,
  `data-testid="remove-selected-units"`, `data-testid="battle-line-draw"`, and
  `data-testid="place-selected-on-map"`.

## 4. Layout math

Add `computeBattleLineLayout` to `battleLine.ts`. It is pure and receives:

```ts
interface BattleLineLayoutInput {
  roster: ArmyRoster;
  states: Readonly<Record<string, UnitPhaseState>>;
  brigadeIds: readonly string[];
  points: readonly Point[];
  frontageByEchelon: FormationFrontages;
  dynamicFrontage: boolean;
}
```

```ts
type BattleLineLayoutResult =
  | {
      ok: true;
      states: Record<string, UnitPhaseState>;
      unitIds: string[];
      brigadeIds: string[];
    }
  | { ok: false; error: string };
```

### 4.1 Brigade order

Brigades are already grouped and ordered by the resolver. Flatten the groups in
that order; the layout never interleaves divisions.

### 4.2 Proportional fill

Use each brigade's **rendered frontage** so dynamic frontage is respected:

- Deployed brigade: `renderedFrontageLength(state.frontageLength, state.effectiveStrength, dynamicFrontage)`.
- New brigade: the same calculation from the current default brigade frontage
  and the roster's derived strength.

Let `totalFrontage` be the sum of rendered frontages and `lineLength` the
polyline length. Each brigade receives a proportional slice of the line:

```text
slotLength_i = lineLength * frontage_i / totalFrontage
centreDistance_i = sum(slotLength_0..i-1) + slotLength_i / 2
```

Place the brigade centre at `pointAtDistance(points, centreDistance_i)` and
rotate it to `tangentAtDistance(points, centreDistance_i)`.

Consequences:

- A line exactly as long as the total frontage places brigades edge-to-edge.
- A longer line spreads them apart.
- A shorter line lets the rectangles overlap, as requested.
- Every centre stays on the drawn line, including on curved lines and
  self-intersections.
- One brigade is centred at the line midpoint and aligned to the local tangent.

### 4.3 Rotations and labels

- Use the local tangent angle in degrees as `rotationDegrees`.
- The existing `isUpsideDownRotation` text flip keeps labels readable.
- A line drawn left-to-right therefore has labels above the rectangles; a line
  drawn right-to-left has labels below. The flip action switches that side.
- Do not normalize rotations to a “readable” angle; the tangent is the source
  of truth.

### 4.4 Command labels

Command labels inside the selected formation tree are deployed when needed and
positioned behind their brigade groups. Unrelated undeployed ancestors are never
added.

A command is eligible for positioning when either:

1. the user explicitly selected it, or
2. every existing brigade descendant of it is part of the line.

Rule 2 prevents a division label from jumping when only two of its three
brigades are drawn into the line. An undeployed command is only added when it
belongs to the selected formation's tree; selecting a corps therefore deploys
its divisions and itself, but not an undeployed army above it.

For each eligible ancestor:

- Centre it behind its own descendant brigade group, not behind the whole line.
- Offset it perpendicular to the group's average tangent, on the side opposite
  the brigades' label side:

```text
behind = { x: -sin(angle), y: cos(angle) }
```

- Offset distances: division 120, corps 240, army 360 world units. These are
  constants in `battleLine.ts`, not document data.
- Rotate the ancestor to the group's average tangent. Text labels continue to
  auto-flip upright.
- Preserve its frontage, strength, status, label override, visibility, and
  notes; only `x`, `y`, and `rotationDegrees` change.

### 4.5 New versus existing brigade states

- Existing deployed brigade: copy the current `UnitPhaseState` and override
  only `x`, `y`, and `rotationDegrees`. Frontage, strength, status, label
  override, visibility, and notes are preserved.
- Undeployed brigade: start from `defaultUnitPhaseState` with the current
  per-echelon default frontage, then override `x`, `y`, and `rotationDegrees`.
  Status stays `reserve` and visibility stays `true`.
- No terrain data, OOB data, or other phases are touched.

### 4.6 Failure cases

The layout returns `{ ok: false, error }` only for:

- no brigades after resolution,
- a degenerate or zero-length line,
- an unknown unit reference.

It does **not** reject a line for being too short; overlap is the intended
result.

## 5. Flip

The flip button operates on `lastBattleLine`.

- For every formation in the line that still has a state in the active phase,
  add 180° to `rotationDegrees` and normalize to `[0, 360)`.
- Positions, order, frontage, strength, status, and all other fields stay
  unchanged.
- The action is one history entry and can be repeated; each press flips again.
- Missing/undeployed formations are skipped. If none remain, the button is
  disabled.
- Clear `lastBattleLine` on:
  - drawing a new line,
  - creating or loading a scenario,
  - `resetForScenario`,
  - undo or redo (both toolbar and keyboard paths).
- The multi-selection inspector also gets a general **Flip 180°** button that
  applies the same rotation math to the current selection. This works without
  a battle line.

## 6. Bulk formation actions

Extend `MultiUnitInspector` with:

- **Frontage (all selected)** — a number field seeded from the primary deployed
  formation. On commit, set the same frontage on every deployed selected
  formation in one `updateUnitStates` call. Undeployed selected units are
  skipped. One undo entry.
- **Flip 180°** — rotate every deployed selected formation by 180° in one
  history entry.
- Existing **Rotation (all selected)** and **Remove selected from map** stay.
- Show a short hint that bulk frontage changes the stored frontage but does not
  re-run the battle line; redraw the line if the spacing needs to change.

These actions are atomic and reuse the current history behavior. Bulk frontage
uses `updateUnitStates`; bulk Flip 180° uses `flipUnitStates`.

## 7. Store and architecture

### 7.1 No schema changes

`UnitPhaseState` already contains `x`, `y`, `rotationDegrees`, and
`frontageLength`. The battle line is transient and adds no fields to
`BattleScenario`, so no schema version bump or migration is required.

### 7.2 Scenario store

Add:

- `applyDeploymentStates(states: Record<string, UnitPhaseState>)` — upsert
  complete states into the active phase in one validated history entry. Used by
  the line tool for both new and existing brigades and for recentred commands.
- `flipUnitStates(unitIds: readonly string[])` — add 180° to every deployed
  state in the list, normalize, and commit one history entry. Returns success
  without a history entry when none of the units are deployed.

Keep `updateUnitStates` for bulk frontage and other partial patches. It
continues to require that every patched unit is already deployed.

### 7.3 Scenario editor store

Add:

- `battleLineActive`
- `lastBattleLine`
- `beginBattleLine()`
- `cancelBattleLine()`
- `setLastBattleLine(line)`
- `clearLastBattleLine()`

`resetForScenario` clears all three. The draft stroke stays in
`ScenarioCanvas`.

### 7.4 Pure module

`features/scenario/battleLine.ts` owns:

- selection resolution and division grouping,
- polyline distance/tangent helpers,
- proportional layout,
- command recentring,
- flip rotation helper,
- constants for command offsets and the degenerate-line threshold.

It imports only models and existing scenario/army helpers, never React or Konva,
so it is unit-testable without mounting the canvas.

## 9. Testing plan

### 9.1 Unit tests — `battleLine.test.ts`

Selection and grouping:

- a division resolves to its three brigades;
- a corps/army resolves to all descendant brigades, grouped by division;
- two brigades from different divisions produce two groups;
- selecting a parent and one of its brigades deduplicates;
- selection order does not affect group order;
- a selection with no brigades returns an empty result;
- hidden-faction brigades are excluded.

Layout:

- three equal-frontage brigades on a straight line are centred at 1/6, 1/2,
  and 5/6 of the line, all at the tangent rotation;
- a longer line creates gaps; a shorter line creates overlap; centres stay on
  the line in both cases;
- a curved polyline follows arc length and local tangents;
- one brigade is centred at the midpoint;
- dynamic frontage changes the proportional slots;
- existing states keep status, strength, label override, visibility, and
  frontage while position/rotation change;
- undeployed brigades receive default states with the configured frontage;
- a degenerate line is rejected;
- no terrain or OOB mutation occurs.

Commands:

- a deployed division whose whole brigade group is included is recentred behind
  the group and rotated to the average tangent;
- a partially included division is not recentred;
- an explicitly selected deployed division is recentred even if the caller
  passes only some of its brigades;
- a selected undeployed division is deployed behind its brigade group;
- a selected corps deploys its divisions and itself but not an undeployed army
  ancestor, while an arbitrary brigade selection deploys no commands;
- nested division/corps/army labels receive increasing offsets.

Flip:

- rotations increase by 180° and normalize;
- positions and order are unchanged;
- missing units are skipped.

### 9.2 Store tests — `scenarioStore.test.ts`

- `applyDeploymentStates` adds new brigades and updates existing ones in one
  history entry; undo restores both the previous positions and the absence of
  the new states; redo reapplies.
- An invalid state is rejected atomically without changing the document or
  history.
- `flipUnitStates` commits one entry, normalizes rotations, and is undoable and
  redoable.
- Flipping an empty/all-undeployed list is a no-op.
- Bulk frontage through `updateUnitStates` is one entry and rejects invalid

- The active phase changes; other phases and the terrain snapshot do not.

   command recentring, flip helper, and unit tests.

   atomic upserts, flips, and undo/redo.
3. **Editor state and inspector** — armed state, last-line metadata, shared

6. **E2E, docs, and full gate** — Playwright coverage, documentation updates,

Each step should leave the app buildable and testable.

## 11. Acceptance criteria

- A selected division, corps, or army can be drawn into a battle line with a
  single freehand stroke.
- All descendant brigades are placed along the line; same-division brigades
  remain contiguous and are ordered deterministically.
- Selecting arbitrary brigades, including two from different divisions, works.
- Already-deployed brigades are repositioned; undeployed brigades are deployed.
- Deployed parent labels recenter behind their brigade groups when the whole
  group is included; selected undeployed commands are deployed with their tree,
  while unrelated undeployed ancestors are not added.
- Short lines produce overlapping brigades, not an error.
- Flip 180° rotates every deployed selected formation in place with no position
  or order change and is one undo entry.
- Flip 180°, Remove selected from map, Draw battle line, and Place on map share
  one inspector Bulk actions section with consistent disabled states.
- Bulk frontage and bulk Flip 180° work on a multi-selection, each as one undo
  entry.
- The drawn line is not persisted as an annotation or document field.
- Terrain, the OOB, and non-active phases are untouched.
- All typecheck, lint, formatting, unit, build, and Playwright checks pass.

## 12. Out of scope

- Saving or editing the battle line as a visible phase annotation (M6).
- Auto-deploying undeployed ancestors outside the selected formation's tree.
- Curved formation lines or post-commit line editing (M8).
- Mid-stroke panning or zooming.
- Automatic facing based on enemy position or faction.
- Scenario persistence and reload recovery (M7).
- Phase management beyond the active phase (M6).

## 13. Edge rules to keep in mind

- Hidden factions are excluded by selection cleanup; hidden individual
  formations keep their visibility when moved.
- A division with only some brigades selected is not recentred.
- A division selected together with its brigades is recentred once.
- Formations with no brigade descendants are ignored by the resolver.
- Command offsets are fixed constants; they are not configurable in this
  milestone.
- The last battle line is transient UI state and is cleared on undo/redo and
  scenario reset.
- Bulk frontage changes stored frontage only; it does not recompute the line.
